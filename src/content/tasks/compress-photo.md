A modern phone photo is 5-12 MB. Most email systems start rejecting attachments somewhere around 20 MB total, and even when they don't, a 50 MB email is a bad look in someone's inbox. The Image Compress tool brings that 5 MB phone photo down to around 400 KB without visible quality loss.

<div class="callout"><strong>When to use this:</strong> Emailing a photo to a coalition partner, posting to social, or attaching to an event invite. Anything where file size matters and "screenshot-of-a-photo" isn't acceptable.</div>

## How to do it

1. Drag the photo into the drop zone (or click and pick a file).
2. Pick an output format: **JPG** for photos, **WebP** for smaller files on the web, **PNG** if you need transparency.
3. The slider next to the preview controls quality. **80** is the default and usually the right answer.
4. Click **Download** to save the smaller version.

## One thing people miss

If you're emailing a photo that has someone's face in it — a candidate headshot, a member testimonial — quality 80 is fine. If you're emailing a chart or screenshot with text, push the quality slider to 90 or use PNG. JPG compression smears small text.
