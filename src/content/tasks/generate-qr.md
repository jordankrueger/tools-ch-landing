QR codes on event flyers, mailers, and yard signs let people register, RSVP, or donate without typing a URL. The QR Generator turns any link into a code you can drop into your design.

<div class="callout"><strong>When to use this:</strong> Putting a QR code on a flyer, a mailer, a yard sign, a presentation slide, or a tabling display.</div>

## How to do it

1. Paste the URL you want the QR to point at. (Use a short link from your action page if the URL is long — fewer dots in the code makes it more reliable when printed small.)
2. Pick a foreground color. Black on white is the safest for any size; you can use your brand color for digital displays, but stick to dark colors so the contrast scanner can find it.
3. Pick **PNG** if you're dropping it into Canva or PowerPoint; **SVG** if you're working in Illustrator or printing at billboard size.
4. Click **Download**.

## One thing people miss

A QR code on a yard sign needs to be at least 1 inch wide to scan from across the lawn. On a half-page mailer, an inch is also a good minimum. On a phone screenshot for social, 200 pixels is enough. If you make it too small, only people standing right next to it can scan it — which defeats the point of putting it on a sign.
